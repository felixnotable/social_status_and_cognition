# Interpretation of the Equal-Weight Contact Composite Results

## Sample

For each relationship domain separately, participants were included only if all six contact-frequency measures were available:

- T1 in-person contact
- T1 phone contact
- T1 written/email contact
- T2 in-person contact
- T2 phone contact
- T2 written/email contact

The complete-six samples were:

| Relationship | N |
|---|---:|
| Children | 7,944 |
| Other relatives | 9,266 |
| Friends | 9,150 |

No cognition or covariate completeness was required at this stage.

## Composite definition

For every eligible participant:

\[
OverallContact_{T1}
=
\frac{InPerson_{T1}+Phone_{T1}+Written_{T1}}{3}
\]

\[
OverallContact_{T2}
=
\frac{InPerson_{T2}+Phone_{T2}+Written_{T2}}{3}
\]

The change score was:

\[
\Delta Contact
=
OverallContact_{T2}-OverallContact_{T1}
\]

Interpretation:

- Positive change = overall contact increased
- Zero = no net change
- Negative change = overall contact decreased

## Main descriptive results

| Relationship | N | T1 mean | T2 mean | Change mean | Change SD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Children | 7,944 | 2.961 | 2.965 | +0.004 | 0.897 |
| Other relatives | 9,266 | 2.191 | 2.166 | -0.025 | 1.172 |
| Friends | 9,150 | 2.585 | 2.569 | -0.016 | 1.103 |

The median change was 0 for all three relationship domains.

## How to interpret the near-zero mean change

The mean change is very close to zero for children, other relatives, and friends. This means that, at the population level, average overall contact frequency changed very little between T1 and T2.

However, a near-zero mean does **not** mean that individual participants did not change.

For example, if one participant increases by +1 and another decreases by -1, the average change is 0 even though both individuals changed substantially.

That is the key distinction between:

- **population-level stability**, and
- **individual-level change**.

## Why the standard deviations matter

The change-score standard deviations were:

- Children: 0.897
- Other relatives: 1.172
- Friends: 1.103

These values are much larger than the corresponding mean changes, which are all close to zero.

This indicates meaningful individual variation around the population average.

Some participants increased their contact substantially, while others decreased their contact substantially.

The observed change ranges were approximately:

- Children: -4.667 to +5.000
- Other relatives: -5.000 to +5.000
- Friends: -5.000 to +5.000

So although the average population remained stable, the underlying participant-level changes were substantial.

## Interpretation of the distribution graphs

### T1 and T2 distributions

The T1 and T2 composite distributions overlap substantially for each relationship domain.

This indicates that the overall population distribution of contact frequency remained fairly stable between T1 and T2.

However, similar population distributions do not imply that the same individuals stayed at the same contact level.

People can move upward and downward while the overall distribution remains almost unchanged.

### Change-score distributions

The change-score distributions are centered around zero.

This means there are participants on both sides of zero:

- participants whose contact decreased,
- participants with little or no change,
- participants whose contact increased.

The distribution around zero is what provides the variation needed for the next regression analysis.

## Differences across relationship domains

The average overall contact score was highest for children, followed by friends, then other relatives.

At T1:

- Children: 2.961
- Friends: 2.585
- Other relatives: 2.191

These values describe **frequency of contact**, not the importance of each relationship for cognition.

A higher mean contact score does not imply that contact with that relationship domain is more strongly associated with cognition.

That question must be tested in the cognition models.

## Why the composite has discrete-looking values

Each modality is scored from 0 to 5.

Because the composite is the mean of three integer-valued modality scores, the possible composite values occur in increments of one-third:

\[
0,\ 0.333,\ 0.667,\ 1.000,\ldots,5.000
\]

Therefore, the histograms naturally show visible peaks at discrete values.

This is expected and does not indicate a coding problem.

## Overall interpretation

The equal-weight contact composites have reasonable variation, and their overall population distributions remain broadly stable between T1 and T2.

At the same time, there is substantial participant-level variation in contact change, with some participants increasing and others decreasing their contact frequency.

This is important because the next longitudinal cognition analysis does not depend on the population mean changing. It depends on whether participants who experienced different amounts of contact change subsequently differed in T3 cognition.

In short:

> Average contact frequency remained relatively stable at the population level, but there was substantial individual-level variation in T1-to-T2 contact change. This provides the variation needed to test whether contact-frequency change is associated with subsequent cognition.
